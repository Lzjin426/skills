# Research Workflow

Use this file before drafting a knowledge-base article.

## Source Mix

Collect enough sources to understand the concept from several angles. A typical article needs 5-10 sources:

- Primary or official source: paper, official docs, standard, textbook chapter, project docs, or original proposal.
- Encyclopedia/reference source: Wikipedia, Baidu Baike, Britannica, MathWorld, Stanford Encyclopedia, or domain wiki.
- Strong tutorial source: a tutorial that explains the learning path well.
- Implementation/example source: code docs, notebooks, examples, or practical case studies.
- Contrasting source: common misconception, limitation, critical discussion, or alternative method.

For current products, libraries, APIs, laws, prices, model capabilities, or fast-changing facts, browse the web and prefer official sources.

## Source Inventory

Keep a compact table while researching:

| Source | Type | Why useful | Structure/visual ideas | Reliability | Reuse notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Reliability levels:

- High: official docs, paper, textbook, standard, maintainer docs.
- Medium: high-quality tutorial, course note, reputable encyclopedia.
- Low: unsourced blog, content farm, copied article, forum answer without evidence.

Reuse notes should say one of:

- Cite only.
- Paraphrase facts.
- Use structure as inspiration.
- Image allowed with license/attribution.
- Image unclear: link or redraw.

## How To Learn From Good Articles

Extract patterns, not expression.

Good things to borrow:

- Entry path: the first problem or contrast used to open the topic.
- Concept sequence: which idea must come before another idea.
- Visual strategy: mechanism diagram, comparison table, running example, heatmap, timeline.
- Depth allocation: where the source slows down and where it intentionally skips detail.
- Terminology map: Chinese term, English term, abbreviation, formula symbol.

Do not copy:

- Sentence wording, paragraph order at a close level, distinctive analogies, original diagrams without permission, tables with the same wording/layout, or screenshots without rights.

## Research Passes

1. Search broad: find encyclopedia pages, official docs, and strong tutorials.
2. Search narrow: search the topic with terms like "intuition", "visual explanation", "derivation", "pitfalls", "implementation", "example", and Chinese equivalents.
3. Compare structures: choose 2-4 sources whose organization is worth learning from.
4. Verify hard facts: definitions, formulas, dates, authors, algorithm steps, constraints, and terminology.
5. Decide the teaching path: what the reader should understand after each section.

## Stop Conditions

Stop researching when:

- You can explain the concept in one sentence, one paragraph, and one diagram.
- You know the prerequisites and the common confusion points.
- You have at least one reliable source for each technical claim that matters.
- You can name which source style you are learning from and what you will improve.

If sources conflict, state the conflict and prefer primary/official sources.
